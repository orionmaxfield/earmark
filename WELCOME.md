# Welcome to the Earmark beta

Thank you for testing Earmark. You're one of the first people to use it, and everything you notice, good or bad, helps make it better before anyone else sees it.

Earmark keeps track of your audiobook listening: your daily streak, your hours, the books you've finished, and medals that level up as you go.

## 1. Open Earmark and add it to your home screen

Open the link from your invite email and log in with your email address. That email is your Earmark account: your listening history is saved to it, so it's there on every phone or computer you sign in on. Then add Earmark to your home screen so it opens like a real app.

**iPhone**

1. Open Earmark in **Safari**.
2. Tap the **Share** button (the square with an arrow pointing up).
3. Scroll down and tap **Add to Home Screen**, then **Add**.
4. From now on, open Earmark from its icon on your home screen.

**Already started in Safari?** There's nothing to move. Open Earmark from your home screen and sign in once more, and everything you've logged comes over automatically. If you'd like an extra safety copy first, open **Settings › Data › Export a backup** in Safari.

**Android**

1. Open Earmark in **Chrome**.
2. Tap **Install app** on Earmark's Home screen. You can also open Chrome's ⋮ menu and choose **Install app** or **Add to Home screen**.
3. From now on, open Earmark from its icon.

## 2. What to try

- **Log your listening every day** with the + button. Your streak starts on day one.
- **Rest days are on by default**, so a missed day here and there won't break your streak. You can turn them off in Settings.
- **Add the books you've finished**, and the ones you're listening to now.
- **Check your medals** in Achievements. They level up from Bronze all the way to Celestial.
- **Bring in your history.** Open **Settings › Data › Import books**. The fastest way is **Quick start**: your lifetime total from the Audible app (Profile, then Stats). On a computer, the **Your Audible library** bookmark brings in every book you own and whether you finished it. For every day you listened, ask Amazon for your data (Your Account › Request Your Data › Audible) and choose **Complete history** when the file arrives. Goodreads, StoryGraph and Libby exports work too. You see everything before it's added, and nothing counts twice.
- **Look at your charts.** On Home, tap Today, This week or This month.

## 3. Your data, on every device

Everything you log is saved on your device first, so Earmark works offline, then synced to your account. Sign in on another phone or computer with the same email and your books, listening and medals are all there. **Settings › Account** shows when it last synced, with a **Sync now** button.

Backups are optional. **Settings › Data › Export a backup** saves a file you can keep as an extra copy, and **Settings › Data › Restore from backup** brings it back.

## Privacy

Your listening data is saved to your account on Earmark's server, tied to your email, so it's available on all your devices. That includes your display name, the books on your shelf, your listening sessions and any notes you add to them, past hours, settings and medals.

It's stored on Earmark's server. The developer can access it to run the service. It's never shared or sold. Deleting your account (**Settings › Account › Delete account**) erases it from the server. The site counts visits with privacy-friendly analytics that use no cookies. Feedback forms are optional, and what you write there comes to me. A backup you export is your own file.

Book searches and lookups go through Earmark's server, which asks Open Library or Google Books, so those services never see you. Earmark's server never collects anything from Audible's catalog or website. Earmark keeps a shared list of books (title, author, narrator, series, length, cover), never who looked them up. Screenshots you add are read once by Anthropic's Claude, through Earmark's server, and aren't kept by Earmark or Anthropic; only the details you choose to add (and a book's cover, cut from the picture) are saved. Import files are read on your device, and the Audible bookmark never sees or asks for your password.
